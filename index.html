The single most important fact

At 1,678 square feet, this investment property is the smallest floor plan Meritage built in Highland Ridge. The community holds 240 homesites ranging from 1,678 to 2,424 square feet. Nothing here is smaller.

That cuts two ways, and both matter to the pricing decision.

It works against us on total rent, because rent is bought and sold by the square foot and there are fewer of them to sell. It works for us on speed, because it produces the lowest monthly rent in the neighborhood. A resident who wants Highland Ridge, Water Spring Elementary and a ten-minute drive to Disney's western gateway, but cannot carry $2,850 a month, has exactly one option in this community — and it is yours. That is a real advantage, and it is the advantage we intend to price into.

It also means there is no perfect comparable. Every leased property we can measure against is larger. So the analysis below adjusts each one down to your size rather than pretending any of them is a twin.

12079 Bracco Street — the closest measurement we have

3 bed / 2.5 bath, 1,770 sq ft, built 2023. Listed June 15 at $2,600 and leased July 14 at full asking price, 29 days. Ninety-two square feet larger than yours and on the same street. This is the anchor for the entire analysis.

Its own history is the lesson. In 2024 the same property launched at $2,800, cut to $2,600 five weeks later, went off market, came back, and finally leased on day 71 — at $2,600. Two years later it launched at $2,600 and leased on day 29. Same rent. Forty-two fewer vacant days, worth roughly $3,600 to that owner. The only thing that changed was the opening number.

It went under contract in three days, lost that applicant, came back, and went under contract again in three days. Two applications inside a week at the same price.

3 bed / 2.5 bath, 2,000 sq ft, built 2023. Listed at $2,850, reduced to $2,800, leased in 47 days — with $300 off the first month on top. It leased for $2,850 back in March 2024. Two and a half years later it took less money and a concession.

4 bed / 3 bath, 2,424 sq ft, built 2024. Launched May 18 at $3,600, cut to $3,400 in late July, leased August 19 — 93 days. The same property leased at $3,600 in 50 days in 2025. This year it took $200 less and 43 days longer.

14340 Braemar Street — still unleased

4 bed / 2.5 bath, 2,062 sq ft, built 2023. Currently asking $3,295 with grounds care and trash included. It has been marketed for 243 days across three separate listing attempts since November 2025, with reductions from $3,500 to $3,400 to $3,300 to $3,295. It has never leased.

Its neighbour at 14387 Braemar is virtually the same property — 32 square feet larger, one year newer — and leased at $2,950. The gap between the two is $345 a month. The 243 vacant days have already cost roughly $26,300 in rent that will never be collected. Even if 14340 eventually leases at its full $3,295 ask, earning back that $26,300 at $345 a month would take more than six years.

Adjusting the comparables to your size

Because every comparable is larger, we measured what the market here actually pays for an additional square foot, then subtracted the difference. Across the five leased properties, each extra square foot is worth about $1.20 a month.

We also tested whether the fourth bedroom carries value of its own, separate from the space it occupies. In this community it does not. The three-bedroom properties earn the same per square foot as the four-bedroom properties, and slightly more. These Meritage plans carve the extra bedroom out of the same upstairs footprint rather than adding to it, and the market is paying for the footprint, not the door count. Your loft does the same work a fourth bedroom does for most residents.

How each adjustment was calculated, and where the method has limits

Square footage. Rent and size across the five leased properties produce a marginal rate of $1.22 per square foot per month. We rounded to $1.20 and applied it to the difference between each comparable and your 1,678 square feet.

Fourth bedroom: no adjustment. Fitting the three-bedroom and four-bedroom results separately produces effectively the same rent at the same size. There is no measurable premium for the fourth bedroom once size is accounted for.

Third full bathroom. 11965 Bracco has three full baths against your two and a half. Adjusted down $50.

Concessions. 14384 Braemar gave $300 off the first month, which is $25 a month across a twelve-month lease. Its achieved rent was therefore $2,775, not $2,800.

Grounds care. 14286 Braemar included lawn care in the rent, a cost its owner absorbs. Adjusted down $110 to state it on the same basis as the properties that do not.

Where this method is conservative. Rent per square foot rises as properties get smaller — a 1,700-square-foot property always earns more per foot than a 2,400-square-foot one. A single straight-line rate cannot capture that curve. Over a small gap it is accurate; over a large one it understates. That is why 14286 (436 square feet larger) and 11965 (746 square feet larger) produce the lowest indications in the table, and why we weight 12079 Bracco — only 92 square feet apart — most heavily. If anything, the true number sits at or a little above $2,450, not below it.

Why this corridor is difficult right now

Highland Ridge sits on Hartzog Road in the southern edge of Horizon West, a few minutes from Disney's western entrance. It is one of the strongest rental locations in Central Florida. It is also one of the most heavily supplied, and the supply is not finished arriving.

Meritage has closed out, but the corridor has not

Highland Ridge itself is built out — 240 homesites, delivered 2022 through 2025, with the builder now gone. That part is finished. What has not finished is Horizon West around it. Ten or more national builders remain actively selling across the corridor: Toll Brothers, M/I Homes, Ashton Woods and Taylor Morrison inside the Ovation master plan alone, with further phases at Waterleigh, Hamlin and the Wellness Way connection into Lake County still releasing. New product a few minutes away, sold with builder incentives, continues to enter the market. A meaningful share of it is bought by investors and goes straight into the rental pool.

Rental supply is being added directly on your road

Flamingo Crossings Village, immediately north of Highland Ridge, holds roughly 10,440 beds of Disney program and cast housing. Alongside it, on eighty acres donated by Disney on Hartzog Road, The Michaels Organization received state permit approval in January 2026 for 1,369 apartment units, more than a thousand of them income-restricted, with the first buildings scheduled to open late this year.

Apartments are not direct competition for a three-bedroom house with a garage and a yard. But roughly 1,400 new doors on the same road absorbs demand from the lower end of the resident pool, and some of the people who would otherwise have stretched into a house will take a new apartment instead.

The sales market is feeding the rental market

This is the part most owners underestimate, and you have already seen it first-hand. Your own purchase closed on October 9, 2025 at $510,000 against a $530,000 asking price, after 83 days on market, with an $18,870 seller credit attached. That is roughly seven percent below ask, on a builder's own inventory, in a community everyone wants.

When properties do not sell, owners rent them instead. Every one of those conversions lands in the same pool your investment property is competing in, and they arrive at unpredictable times with owners who are often willing to take less than they hoped for because they have already been disappointed once. We have watched precisely this dynamic reshape the Lake Nona corridor over the past eighteen months. Horizon West is the same story with a different exit off the 429.

The demand side — why this location still works

None of the above means this is a weak location. It is the opposite, and it is why the strategy is to price into the demand rather than wait for it.

Employment. Disney's western gateway on Western Way is minutes away, and the 429 connects to I-4, the Turnpike and the broader Orlando employment base without touching surface roads.

Retail and services. Flamingo Crossings Town Center, Disney-owned and immediately north, anchors with Target and carries more than forty retail and dining tenants, with a further 237,000-square-foot village centre phase planned. Before it opened, this part of Horizon West had to drive to Hamlin or Windermere for groceries.

Schools. Water Spring Elementary, Water Spring Middle and Horizon High School. School zoning is the single most reliable driver of family rental demand in Central Florida, and this zoning is a genuine asset.

Parks. Horizon West Regional Park, 215 acres north of Hamlin, opened its first phase in early 2026, with a second phase including a splash pad and kayak launch under construction since June.

The demand is there. It is simply being asked to choose between more options than it had two years ago, and price is how it chooses.

What vacancy actually costs

Vacancy is the most expensive line item in rental ownership, and it is the only one that is invisible — there is no invoice for it, so it does not feel like money. It is.

At anything under a $200 monthly premium, a single extra vacant month cannot be repaid inside the lease term at all — the resident moves out before the maths catches up. And the premium only pays if you actually get it. 14340 Braemar has been chasing roughly $345 a month more than its neighbour since November, has forgone about $26,300 in rent doing it, and has not yet been paid the premium once.

The reduction trap

Listing high with a plan to reduce later sounds cautious. It is the opposite. Prospective residents watch the days-on-market counter on Zillow, and a property that has been sitting reads to them as a property with something wrong with it — so they skip past it without ever asking. Meanwhile the calendar moves against you: family relocations slow after August, and sale-to-rental conversions keep arriving through the autumn. A price that looks competitive in September looks ordinary by late October and expensive by November. Anyone who lists high intending to reduce is reducing into more competition than they started with, not less.

How we will run the launch

We do not give owners an expected time-to-lease. There is no honest way to produce that number, and a range invented to sound reassuring is worse than none. We do not have a crystal ball. We have a strategy, and it is measured rather than guessed.

We list at $2,450 and we measure the first five days — enquiries, showings scheduled, showings actually attended, applications started. If that activity is not strong enough for us to choose from a pool of qualified applicants, we reduce to $2,375. Not a token $25 that nobody notices, but a number that moves the property into a new set of search results and signals to the people already watching it that something changed.

If day five to day twelve still does not produce a pool, we step to $2,295 and keep reassessing. We want to be clear about that last figure: it is a step, not a bottom. Only the market knows where the bottom is. Our commitment is to keep reacting to what it tells us rather than digging in on a number we picked in advance.

The purpose of the pool is selection, not acceptance. One applicant is not a decision — it is a hope. Four applicants is a decision, and it is the only position from which we can decline the two who should be declined and still get your property leased.

The four things inside our control

  • Price. Decisive, and the only lever that works quickly. Everything below is a tiebreaker between two properties a resident is already considering.
  • Grounds care included. The fastest lease in the set — six days — came with lawn care in the rent. On a 4,250-square-foot lot the yard is small and the cost is modest. We will confirm the exact monthly figure with our vendor before you commit to it. It will not rescue a wrong price, but between two similar properties at a similar rent it is the one residents pick.
  • Flooring. Residents increasingly expect hard surfaces and choose those properties first. Your first floor is ceramic tile; the bedrooms and loft were delivered in carpet. If that carpet is original from 2022 and shows wear, replacing it before launch buys both a higher rent and a faster one. If it is clean, we present it as is.
  • Appliances. A complete package — refrigerator, washer and dryer — is common among the properties that leased here, though it did not save the one asking $1.60 per foot. Worth having. Not worth paying for a wrong price with.

We market unfurnished. Every property that leased in Highland Ridge this summer was unfurnished, and it is the right answer for a long-term lease in this submarket regardless — it widens the resident pool, removes an entire category of move-out dispute, and keeps your investment in the twelve-month family market rather than the short-stay one.

In summary

Your investment property is the smallest floor plan in a 240-home community, on a street where five larger properties leased this summer between $1.35 and $1.47 per square foot and nothing leased above that. Adjusted to your size, those five indicate a median of $2,451.

Horizon West is a strong rental location that is currently absorbing a great deal of supply at once — builders still releasing across the corridor, roughly 1,400 apartment units arriving on Hartzog Road this year, and a soft sales market steadily converting would-be sellers into landlords. Your own purchase, at seven percent under ask with a seller credit, is direct evidence of that last one.

We recommend listing at $2,450 with grounds care included, measuring hard at day five, and reducing decisively rather than waiting. The goal is not to find a resident. It is to generate enough qualified interest that we can choose the right one, and to do it before the calendar and the competition make the same rent harder to get.

The most expensive number in this document is not $2,450 or $2,550. It is the $26,300 sitting at 14340 Braemar Street.

We are glad to walk through any part of this with you, and to revisit it at any point once the property is live.
